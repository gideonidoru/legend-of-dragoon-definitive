# Engine pacing contract

Timing belongs to the domain that advances it. Display refresh, queued audio and a previous callback's deadline must not silently change that domain's speed.

| Domain | Clock and intended behavior | Verification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gameplay, battle, submaps, world map, menus and transitions | Monotonic fixed steps at `60 / vsyncMode × gameSpeedMultiplier`. Native divisors 1, 2 and 3 give 60, 30 and 20 Hz; explicit speeds 1–16 accelerate gameplay. Presentation may run less frequently. Frame skipping requests the base presentation rate; input and simulation keep the multiplied rate. | All 48 state/speed combinations at six presentation schedules, including the complete production `onDraw` path; actual gameplay timing setter. |
| Script VM, effects, transitions and native GPU commands | Each authoritative step invokes the complete game callback. VM frames, object tickers, native GPU start/end, CLUT animation and engine counters advance per step. Pause stops simulation; manual stepping advances once. Intermediate draw queues are replaced, and zero-step presentations retain a complete scene. | Actual script VM/ticker/render pause/resume/stop checks; queue/object/CLUT/scissor/notification/UI ownership checks and callback handoffs. |
| Native model animations | Existing keyframe/interpolation ownership advances with gameplay steps. Custom HD geometry keeps native animation timing. | Actual model loader/animator, complete keyframe cycles at interpolation strides 2, 4 and 6; source pose probes. |
| Dabas hardware | Its dynamic hardware callback rate is neutral to game fast-forward and independent of display caps. Its separate timer produces 44.1 kHz mono PCM. Ordered bounded handoff backpressures that producer instead of dropping samples or waiting in the renderer; pause and shutdown release the correct scope. | Actual hardware callback entry/change/exit across all gameplay rates, capped presentation and saved-state restoration; PCM ordering, producer backpressure, pause/close/device cancellation. |
| Startup and loading presentation | Elapsed-time eye/loading fades and the exact integral of the original clipped-sine hue animation preserve the native 60 Hz visual rate at other callback frequencies. | Enhanced films and launch logo | Media follows actually played 48 kHz stereo samples. Queued buffers are not counted as played. Stalls discard stale platform deadlines without replaying an unbounded callback burst. | Virtual-clock rate handoffs, repeated assignments, stall limits, rejected rates and monotonic wrap. |

## Recovery and handoffs

Gameplay recovery retains at most 250 ms of elapsed debt. Hardware timers at 1–4 Hz retain one complete interval plus 250 ms of bounded remainder, so every accepted rate can become due without losing phase between presentations. One presentation runs at most 256 steps and starts no further step once catch-up has used 50 ms; a single authoritative step is not interrupted. Remaining bounded debt carries forward. Excess elapsed time is discarded to preserve responsiveness after suspension or sustained overload. `RenderEngine.simulationTiming()` exposes rate, pending debt, completed steps, discarded time and work-budget exhaustion. No fixed-step clock can provide throughput that the CPU cannot sustain.

Rate changes and callback ownership changes discard old-domain debt. Saved callbacks carry their own gameplay/hardware rate so a movie or Dabas scope cannot restore the previous domain at the wrong cadence. Input press/repeat pulses stay latched until a step consumes them; ordinary brief taps survive between presentations. Focus/input cancellation removes stale edges, and synchronous release events observe the cancelled state. Held buttons and axes remain platform-owned.

The platform scheduler formerly retained a roughly 66 ms movie deadline when switching from 15 Hz playback to 960 Hz gameplay. It now re-arms the new approximately 1 ms deadline. Repeated assignments remain no-ops because resetting them each callback would introduce drift.

## Acceptance limits

The consolidated integration is still under final review and exact-source native/package checks. The reports record immutable source checkpoints and distinguish source, CPU/headless, native CI and public-download evidence. Original-film CPU decoding is complete, but enhanced film playback tests sample the shipped decoders rather than claiming a full-duration viewing of every film. None of these fixtures proves physical Steam Deck performance, a full campaign, perceptual A/V acceptance or the appearance of every framebuffer-feedback effect. The public development download remains held until the combined release gates pass.
